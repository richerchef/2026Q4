Performance intelligence framework03 / Gather
03 / Trust before insight

Automate the boring checks.
Escalate the suspicious.

Automated validation

  • Are expected sessions, laps and channels present?
  • Do timestamps, lap boundaries and units align?
  • Are there gaps, stuck sensors, impossible values or duplicated records?
  • Did a schema or naming change break an assumption?
  • Can each output be reproduced from a known data version?

Route the result

PASS

Continue through the standard analysis pipeline.

WARNING

Produce results with a visible caveat and flag for review.

FAIL

Block affected outputs or mark them invalid; notify the owner.

A failed quality check should never silently become a confident-looking chart or report.

Performance intelligence framework04 / Validate
04 / The repeatable engine

Automated analysis finds the needles.

Once data is validated, standard jobs run consistently after every session. They calculate known measures, compare against reference points and surface exceptions for people to interpret.

Example analysis families

Performance: lap / sector deltas, corner-phase comparison, speed traces, time loss attribution.

Tyres: degradation trends, warm-up behaviour, stint comparisons and sensitivity to conditions.

Reliability: channel excursions, repeated anomalies, component trends and fault precursors.

Operations: pit-stop performance, run-plan completion, prediction-vs-outcome.

Reports should answer questions, not just show charts.

01

What changed?

Compare with target, simulation, teammate, prior run or historical baseline.

How important?

Automated report generation can assemble standard plots, tables, annotations and concise summaries into a consistent event pack — with links back to the analysis and its data.

06 / Human-led deep dive

Automation spots the signal.
Experts ask the next question.

Standard analysis is deliberately broad and repeatable. Bespoke investigation is focused and iterative: an engineer notices something unexpected, frames a hypothesis and works with data specialists to test it.

1 · ObserveA corner exit is slower than expected.
2 · HypothesiseTyre state, line, setup or energy?
3 · TestAlign laps and compare relevant channels.
4 · ChallengeCheck confounders and alternative causes.

Performance intelligence framework07 / Investigate
07 / Review and decide

The meeting is where insight becomes commitment.

Typical review agenda

  • Performance summary: targets versus actual, key deltas and confidence.
  • Exceptions: unexpected results, reliability concerns and unresolved questions.
  • Investigation read-outs: evidence, root-cause candidates and alternatives.
  • Decisions: what to change, test, monitor or accept.
  • Actions: named owner, due date, expected outcome and verification method.
ACTION REGISTER · EXAMPLE

From finding to follow-through

Finding: repeatable loss in a specific corner phase.

Action: compare setup variants in simulation and validate next session.

Owner: responsible performance / vehicle engineer.

Success check: agreed metric, comparison baseline and review date.

Not every finding creates an action. Some are monitored, accepted as trade-offs, or closed when evidence is insufficient.

Performance intelligence framework09 / Timeline
09 / Make it reliable

The system is only as good as its feedback loop.

DATA

Traceable

Versioned inputs, definitions, assumptions and reproducible outputs. Make it possible to recreate last week's conclusion.

MODELS

Monitored

Track prediction error, drift, false alarms and known blind spots. Record when a model is not appropriate to use.

ACTIONS

Closed-loop

Review whether agreed actions happened and whether the expected effect was observed. Feed the result into future analysis.

Measure the process

Time from flag to trusted report · findings reviewed · actions closed · repeat issues · prediction accuracy · engineer time saved.

Protect judgement

Automation prioritises and accelerates the work. Engineers remain accountable for interpretation, trade-offs and decisions.
